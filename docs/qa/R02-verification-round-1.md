## 1. ⚠️ A CORRECTION TO ONE OF MY OWN CORRECTIONS — L008

`L008-R01` corrected the discovery pass, which had said Jean Pesijn founded the hofje after his
only daughter died of plague. `R01` replaced that with: **the hofje was founded in 1683 by his
widow Marie de Lannoy.**

**That correction was itself partly wrong.** The fuller record:

- The hofje was founded by **Jean Pesijn *and* his wife Marie de Lannoy, together**, and they
  set it out **in their testament**.
- After several plague epidemics they decided, in a **first testament of July 1655**, to
  establish an almshouse.
- **After their only daughter — also named Marie — died of the plague**, that decision was
  **confirmed in their final testament of October 1655**.
- **Construction began in 1683; the first residents moved in in 1686.**
- It was intended for **elderly married couples of the Walloon community**, who could live
  there free and with support.
- The testament stipulated ***preuven***: fixed annual or weekly distributions of money and
  goods "that made daily life bearable".

### What this means
| Claim | Discovery pass | `R01` | **`R02` — verified** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who founded it | Jean Pesijn | Marie de Lannoy, as widow | **Both, jointly, by testament** |
| The plague daughter | Yes | "attached to the wrong person, re-verify" | **True. Their only daughter, also called Marie** |
| Date | 1683 building, 1686 residents | 1683 founding | **Testaments 1655; built 1683; occupied 1686** |

The discovery pass was closer to right than `R01` was. `R01` over-corrected on a partial source.

**The honest formulation for visitors:** *in 1655, after their only daughter Marie died of
plague, Jean Pesijn and Marie de Lannoy wrote into their will that their money should house
elderly couples of the Walloon church. The houses were built in 1683. People still live in them.*

**Marie de Lannoy remains a named woman in the MVP** — but as **co-author of a will**, not as a
widow acting alone. That is a smaller claim and a true one.

**Process note:** this is exactly what `R02` is for, and exactly why master §0.12 says nothing
is DONE because it looks good. An `R01` correction that felt decisive was built on one source.
**`R02` must not be skipped for any location on the grounds that `R01` "already checked".**

### New — and it fills `R01`'s biggest gap
`L009-R01` recorded that nobody had looked for **who the dead were**. Two are now named:

- **Professor Adriaan Kluit (1735–1807)** — professor of history, constitutional law and statistics.
- **Professor Jean Luzac (1746–1807)** — professor of literature.

Two university professors among 151 dead, in a university city, killed by a passing barge.
That converts an abstraction into people, and it links L009 to the university cluster.

### New — the cause, in detail
Eyewitnesses stated that **cooking was under way on board just before the explosion**: a
**bucket of peeled potatoes on deck**, and **the smell of fried fish from an open hatch**. How
the ship actually exploded **has never been fully clarified**.

The potatoes are the detail that makes it real. It was an ordinary afternoon and someone was
making dinner.

### New — and the most quotable fact in the file
**Only thirteen fragments of the ship were ever found.**

## 3. L012 — THE 312-YEAR HOLE IS CLOSED

`L012-R01` flagged as its key question: *what happened between 1574 and 1886?* Answer:

> **Since 1574, a thanksgiving service has been held every year on 3 October in the Pieterskerk.**

So there are **two traditions, not one**, with different ages and different homes:

| Tradition | Since | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **The thanksgiving service** | **1574 — unbroken** | **The Pieterskerk (→ L006)** |
| **The herring and white bread distribution** | **1886** | **De Waag** |

This is a better answer than either of the versions `R01` could choose between. The
commemoration *is* continuous since 1574; the **food ritual** is a Victorian civic invention on
top of it.

**And it connects L012 to L006**, which is already an MVP stop. The oldest continuous element of
Leidens Ontzet happens inside a building the route already visits.

### How the 1886 tradition came about
- The trigger was the **unveiling of a statue of Van der Werf in 1884**.
- Enthusiasm was such that **N. Brouwer**, a teacher and member of the Committee of Public
  Gatherings, took the initiative to found an association.
- The **3 October Vereeniging was formally founded on 13 May 1886** and has organised the
  festivities since.

A 19th-century statue unveiling produced the festival that now defines the city's calendar.
That is a *sourced* story about how civic memory gets manufactured, and it is more interesting
than "they have always done this".

## 4. L002 — TWO SUPERLATIVES SUPPORTED, ONE STILL OPEN

| Claim | **`R02` status** |
|---|---|
| "The Visbrug is Leiden's **oldest bridge**; the first bridge here was 12th-century" | **Supported** by two independent sources. ⚠️ Still needs an **institutional** source before publication (DEC-013) |
| "With the **Sint Jansbrug**, the only fixed bridge crossing of the Rhine **between Utrecht and the North Sea**" | **Supported** by two independent sources. Same caveat |
| "**Nearly 900 years** of weekly market" | ⚠️ **Still unsourced.** Do not use |

### New context
- The **Sint Jansbrug** crosses the **Oude Rijn less than 100 metres north** of the Visbrug.
- **Because the bridge mattered so much, all the places in the region around Leiden had to
  contribute to its upkeep.**
- Fishermen and farmers came from far around to trade here.

The maintenance detail is the good one: a bridge so important that the surrounding villages
were made to pay for it. That is a **verifiable claim about regional power**, and far stronger
than "the oldest bridge".
