# R02 source verification — round 4

Checked: 27 September 2026. Scope: unresolved R01 claims that can be checked
against current, institutional or specialist sources without archive access.

## Outcome

| Location | Result | Visitor-safe wording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L001 Burcht | **Resolved.** The 1996 South Holland archaeological chronicle describes research into the water cellar and reservoirs and states that they supplied the fish fountain from 1693. | The Burcht reservoirs and water cellar supplied the Vismarkt fountain from 1693. |
| L002 Vismarkt | **Partly resolved.** A scholarly Oud Leiden yearbook calls the Visbrug and Sint Jansbrug Leiden's oldest Rhine crossing and documents the regional maintenance duty. It does not support “nearly 900 years of market on this exact spot” or the broader Utrecht-to-North-Sea wording. | “An early and regionally important Rhine crossing.” Do not use the 900-year or Utrecht-to-sea claims. |
| L003 Koornbrug | **Already resolved in round 3.** The reopening was confirmed there for 14 May 2026. This pass also found the municipality's original schedule and subsequent area works, but no newer closure notice. | The bridge reopened after the restoration. Recheck current municipal notices and the site itself immediately before recording or visiting. |
| L006 Pieterskerk | **Resolved in part.** The Cultural Heritage Agency's building history confirms the March 1512 collapse and explicitly says the cause cannot be determined precisely. It does not establish the 100–110 metre range used in secondary sources. | The upper west tower collapsed in March 1512; its precise cause is unknown. Omit a precise height unless a cited architectural reconstruction is added. |
| L008 Jean Pesijnhofje | **Still partly open.** Existing secondary research supports the joint charitable intent and plague-daughter story, but this pass did not locate a digitised primary testament. | Attribute the daughter's death and its role in the decision to the local secondary source; do not present grief as a proven sole motive. |
| L009 Buskruitramp | **Still open.** University material confirms date, cargo scale and impact, but this pass did not identify everyone aboard. Modern institutional sources also vary between 151, about 160 and 161 deaths. | Do not name anyone as being aboard. Say that sources differ slightly on the death toll and use 151 only with that caveat. |
| L010 Hortus | **Resolved in part.** The official Hortus history confirms Clusius's 1593 arrival, his important tulip collection and successful cultivation. It does not confirm the attractive 1596/97 theft-to-Bollenstreek chain. | State only that bulbs were stolen; omit the exact night, thief and direct origin story of the Bollenstreek. |
| L012 Leidens Ontzet | **Resolved for annual continuity.** The official organiser states that a remembrance service has been held every year since 1574, including during the Second World War. The herring-and-white-bread distribution in its organised form dates from 1886. | Keep the service and food tradition separate and date each one explicitly. |

## Archive/contact work that remains

These questions cannot honestly be closed by more general web searching:

1. Consult or request the 1655 Pesijn–De Lannoy testament.
2. Ask Erfgoed Leiden for the crew/cargo records of *Delfs Welvaaren*.
3. Request an archival or architectural source for the Pieterskerk tower height and appearance.
4. Check the Koornbrug physically and against current municipal access notices immediately before filming.
5. Create per-claim `SourceRecord`s as content moves from research notes into final audio or video.

Until then, the safe wording above is the publication boundary. “Not found” is
not evidence that an attractive claim is false, but it is a reason not to state
that claim as established fact.
