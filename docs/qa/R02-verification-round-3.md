## 1. L003 KOORNBRUG — OPEN. UNBLOCKED.

**The bridge reopened on Wednesday 14 May 2026**, after roughly six months of work.

`R01` and `R02` round 2 left this as *"the scheduled end date has passed, so it is probably
finished, and this project does not publish probably."* It is now confirmed, and the story
improved on the way:

- What looked like straightforward maintenance became **an extensive restoration**: the condition
  of the **pillars and foundation was significantly worse than the municipality had realised**,
  and specialists had to be brought in.
- It ran past its 24 April target and finished in **May 2026**.
- **Heavy traffic is now permanently barred** from the bridge and directed to the **Visbrug**
  (which is L002 — the diversion runs between two MVP stops).

**New usable fact:** the 1824 canopy nearly outlived its own foundations, and nobody knew until
they opened it up. For a location whose whole pitch is *look up at how this thing works*, "it was
closer to collapse than anyone realised" is a good, sourced, recent beat.

## 2. ⭐ L005 GRAVENSTEEN — ACCESSIBLE, AND ERFGOED LEIDEN IS TEMPORARILY IN IT

`R01` recorded the interior as inaccessible: the university left in 2024 and the building was
between tenants. **That is out of date, and the new position is much better.**

- **Pieterskerk Leiden has run guided tours of the Gravensteen several times a week since
  summer 2025** — described as *"a place you don't normally just enter"*.
- The reading room of the **Hoogheemraadschap van Rijnland** remains open to visitors.
- The municipality is still seeking a **long-term** tenant; the current use is explicitly temporary.

### And the part that matters beyond this location
> **Erfgoed Leiden en Omstreken has temporarily moved into the Gravensteen**, while its own
> Boisotkade premises are renovated.

**The project's single most important source institution is, right now, physically housed inside
one of the fourteen MVP locations.** Five of the seven blockers left after round 2 end at their
archive, and `R02` round 3's standing recommendation was that this had "stopped being a research
task and become a contact task". It is now also a *walk-in* task.

Other tenants include Karl Beerenfenger Projecten, Douwe Baert, Michel Lausberg, Bastiaan Bakker
Events and Bot Uitgevers.

**Effect on L005:** it moves from an exterior-only stop to one with **a real, bookable interior
route** — and the 1556 cell block, which `R01` called out as potentially transformative, is
suddenly worth asking about. **Ask the Pieterskerk what the tour actually shows.**

⚠️ All of this is **temporary by design**. `PracticalInfo` must not present a transitional
arrangement as permanent, and this needs re-checking before launch.

| **Price** | **Free**, for Dutch and international visitors alike |
| **Groups** | Weaving and spinning demonstrations outside normal hours, possibly for a fee |
| **Contact** | leidswevershuis@gmail.com · 071-3010687 · 06-23203423 |

### Accessibility — and how the product must phrase it
The museum's own statement is: **wheelchair accessible, but not entirely.** A **narrow hallway**
and **high thresholds** between rooms make it **poorly accessible for wheelchairs**. **Only
someone with a narrow wheelchair and an accompanying person can view the ground floor.**

That is precisely the level of honesty master §20 requires, and the app should carry it close to
verbatim rather than flattening it to an icon. "Partially accessible" would be a lie of omission
here; a visitor needs to know it is the **ground floor only**, with a **narrow** chair, and **not
alone**.

### Two consequences for routes
1. **Three hours a day, six days a week.** Any route containing L013 is effectively an afternoon
   route, Tuesday to Sunday. `RT003` ("People who came here") is built on it.
2. **It is free.** That materially raises its value — `LDN-SCORE-001` scored it 58 partly on
   friction that turns out not to exist. Not a reason to re-score, but worth noting at the next
   MVP revision.

## 4. L008 JEAN PESIJNHOFJE — THE ETIQUETTE, FROM THE SOURCES

`R01` §6 made access a hard constraint and said the answer might be "look from the gate, don't
enter". **That is very close to what the sources actually say:**

- It remains **an inhabited hofje** in the 21st century.
- **The large entrance door is open and you can walk in.**
- **Most residents don't mind people looking — but always respect privacy.**
- **"Best to stay at the entrance and view the hofje from there."**
- **"Don't make too much noise. These are real places of rest in the city; keep it that way."**

No published opening hours were found. The foundation should be contacted directly.

**The product's instruction is therefore settled:** *stand at the gate, look in, keep your voice
down, don't go further.* That is not a watered-down version of the experience — the **1683 gate
building, its central chimney and the regents' chamber above it** are all visible from exactly
there, which is where `R01` said the location works from anyway.

⚠️ **Volume is the risk this product creates.** "Most residents don't mind" is a statement about
today's numbers. An app that routes a continuous stream of visitors into a small courtyard would
change that, and the foundation should be consulted **before** launch, not after complaints.
This belongs on the board as a live concern, not a footnote.
