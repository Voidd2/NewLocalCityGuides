## 1. THE SHORT ANSWER

A church with no tower, because the tower fell down one night in 1512 and the city could not
afford to rebuild it. Almost everyone who matters in this app is buried inside.

---

## 2. THE NIGHT OF 4–5 MARCH 1512

The single best-documented event at this location, and the one that explains what the visitor
is looking at.

- The tower **collapsed in the night of 4–5 March 1512**.
- It was nicknamed **"Coningh der Zee"** — King of the Sea.
- It stood **100 to 110 metres** high and would have been among the **tallest church towers in
  the Netherlands**.
- After the collapse the **nave was extended** to what is now the church's **western gable**,
  and the **pillars and arches from the transept were moved** to where the tower had stood.
- **It was never rebuilt — for lack of money.**

So the church's present shape is a **repair**. The west end of the building is where a
collapsed tower used to be, patched with parts moved from elsewhere in the same church. A
visitor standing at the west front is looking at the scar.

⚠️ "Would have been one of the tallest in the Netherlands" is a **counterfactual superlative**.
DEC-013 applies: `R02` must source the height figure, and the claim should be phrased as what
the sources support, not as a record.

## 4. THE PLACE, PEOPLE AND ACTION (master §9)

- Leiden's principal medieval parish church, 14th–15th century.
- After the Reformation its use changed; it is now deconsecrated and serves as an exhibition
  and events venue, and presents the **Pilgrim** history (see L007).
- **Action:** worship, burial, civic ceremony — and, in 1512, a structural catastrophe.

⚠️ `R04` gap: what ordinary parish life here actually involved — who came, when, what they did —
is not established and is exactly what master §9 says research must answer beyond dates and
architects' names.

## 5. NARRATIVE

**Candidate hook:** *"Look for the tower. There isn't one."*

- **Conflict:** ambition versus money. They built the tallest thing around and could not afford
  to put it back.
- **Transformation:** Catholic parish church → Protestant church → deconsecrated venue.
- **Unexpected detail:** the west end is made of **parts of the church's own transept**, moved.
- **Human perspective:** the people underneath the floor are the cast of this entire app.

## 8. R05 — UNCERTAINTY REGISTER

### VERIFIED
- The tower **collapsed in the night of 4–5 March 1512** and was **never rebuilt, for lack of funds**.
- The nave was **extended to the present west gable**; transept pillars and arches were **moved**
  to the former tower position.
- The tower was nicknamed **"Coningh der Zee"**.
- **John Robinson**, **Jan Steen**, **Herman Boerhaave**, **Jacobus Arminius**, **Rembert
  Dodoens** and **Rembrandt's parents** are buried or commemorated here.

### PROBABLE — confirm in `R02`
- The **100–110 m** height. A range, not a figure, and the basis for it is unclear.
- Whether it "would have been one of the tallest church towers in the Netherlands" —
  counterfactual, DEC-013.

### NOT ESTABLISHED
- **What the tower actually looked like.** Blocking for any video.
- Whether the **moved pillars** are identifiable to a visitor.
- Whether individual graves are **marked and findable**.
- **Why** it collapsed. A named cause would be a strong fun fact; an invented one would be a
  serious failure.
- Ordinary parish life (`R04`).
