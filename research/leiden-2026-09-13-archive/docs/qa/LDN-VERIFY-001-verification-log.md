Tourism copy is full of "oldest", "first" and "only". Each one was checked independently.

| C020 | Oude Sterrewacht is *"the world's first purpose-built university observatory"* | **DISPUTED — do not use** | Leiden University describes it as the *oldest existing* university observatory (observing since 1633, in this building since 1861). ## 3. UPGRADES FOUND WHILE VERIFYING

Not the goal of the pass, but material:

- **C005 Gravensteen** `MEDIUM` → `HIGH`. Built early 13th c. as a refuge tower of the counts
  of Holland. **1463**: executions moved here from the Blauwe Steen; spectators watched from a
  **columned gallery that still exists**. 1556 cell block (intact), 1598 tuchthuis, 1655
  spin- en rasphuis, 1672 vierschaar. **Last execution in Leiden: 1856, Pieterskerkhof —
  Adrianus Blom, for the murder of eighteen-year-old Maria van Velsen.** University left the
  building in 2024, so interior access must be re-checked.
- **C007 Stadhuis** `MEDIUM` → `HIGH`. Fire **12 February 1929**. Façade **1597** by **Lieven
  de Key**, Obernkirchener sandstone, **longest Renaissance façade in the Netherlands**,
  rebuilt from its own remains. Design for the new building commissioned July **1934** from
  **Cornelis Jonke Blaauw**; in use 1940, council chamber 1945, citizens' hall 1948.
- **C010 Koornbrug** `MEDIUM` → `HIGH`, **with a correction**: the roofs are **1824**, not
  18th-century. Name from **1443** (first grain market), present bridge **1642**, roofs 1824
  by city architect **Salomon van der Paauw**. Ceiling holes are ventilation; the roofing
  carries Leiden's **crossed keys surrounded by ears of grain**. Three dates that must not be
  blurred into one.

## 5. FALSE TRAILS RULED OUT

Recorded so no future agent spends time on them, or worse, publishes them:

- **Aletta Jacobs** — first Dutch woman to graduate from university and first woman doctor —
  was born in Sappemeer and studied at **Groningen**. **Not a Leiden story.**
- **Anna Maria van Schurman** — the first female student in the Netherlands, admitted in
  **1636**, who attended lectures from behind a curtain — was at **Utrecht**. **Not Leiden.**

Both are frequently attached to any Dutch university-town narrative. Neither belongs here.
Leiden's real women's story is C057 (spinsters and factory children) and C059 (a movement
that had to start in professors' drawing rooms because the lecture halls were closed).
