# Competitor and pricing research for a Leiden city guide

**Task:** `COMPETITOR-PRICING-001`  
**Research date:** 2026-09-17  
**Status:** Ready for review  
**Currency note:** Prices are shown in the currency and storefront region of the cited source. No
exchange-rate conversion is used. App ratings and prices can change by country and date.

## Decision summary

The direct competitive set is split into three different buyer expectations:

1. **Large catalogue apps** sell breadth, offline maps and convenience. GPSmyCity charges an annual
   subscription; izi.TRAVEL mixes free, ad-supported, subscription and per-tour access.
2. **Produced audio tours** sell a single, authored experience. Vidi Guides commonly charges about
   GBP 5.99–12.99 for one tour and GBP 99.99 for a global bundle.
3. **Leiden alternatives** range from EUR 6 volunteer-led walks and EUR 6.95 paper routes to
   private guided experiences at EUR 20 per person or EUR 134.92 per group.

For this product, catalogue size is the wrong contest. A defensible position is a carefully sourced,
offline-capable Leiden product in Dutch, English and German, with date-aware routes and transparent
visitor guidance. The first price test should use a **EUR 6.99 single-route offer** and a **EUR
12.99–14.99 Leiden pass** containing all launch routes. These are hypotheses for user testing, not
approved prices. The lower point sits close to local self-guided and volunteer alternatives; the
city pass remains below a single commercial guided place while supporting higher production value.

### Important izi.TRAVEL discrepancy

The izi.TRAVEL website still states that use of the system and mobile guide app is free and that the
professional CMS has no hidden costs [C12], [C13]. Current Apple and Google listings show ads,
subscriptions and in-app purchases [C01], [C03]. The creator FAQ also states a 50% publisher share
after taxes for paid tours [C02]. Any distribution decision must therefore use a written current
commercial offer from izi.TRAVEL rather than the older marketing copy.

## Pricing interpretation

### Price anchors a buyer will see

- **EUR 0:** museum apps, tip-based tours before the tip, free platform content.
- **EUR 3–7:** museum audio add-ons, Gilde Leiden and the VVV Rembrandt Route.
- **EUR 7–13:** many single produced audio tours and the VVV puzzle booklet.
- **EUR 15–21:** annual global app access or one commercial guided-tour place.
- **EUR 100+:** private group guide or broad global bundles.

This makes a single Leiden route above EUR 10 difficult without a distinctive promise and sample.
Conversely, pricing the full city below EUR 5 would place it beside subsidised museum add-ons and
would not signal the cost of original multilingual production.

### Recommended experiments

These are test proposals, not a locked commercial decision:

1. **Free preview:** map overview plus one complete stop, with no account required.
2. **Single route:** A/B test EUR 5.99 against EUR 6.99. Use permanent access and allow offline use.
3. **Leiden pass:** A/B test EUR 12.99 against EUR 14.99 for all launch routes in NL/EN/DE.
4. **Household mode:** allow one purchaser to use the experience with a small group on one device;
   do not force each family member to buy separately.
5. **No launch subscription:** a recurring plan makes little sense for a one-city MVP. Revisit only
   after a multi-city catalogue creates repeat annual value.

The checkout should display exactly what access remains after the trip. Auto-renewing trials are a
visible friction point in competitor reviews and are unnecessary for the Leiden MVP.

## Product and positioning implications

### Required parity

- Offline route data, audio and essential images.
- Clear map, live position and recovery after a visitor leaves the route.
- Audio plus readable text; captions/transcripts for every spoken item.
- Dutch, English and German available before purchase, with no mixed-language gaps.
- A short preview that demonstrates voice, map behaviour and historical sourcing.
- Honest duration and walking distance, plus accessibility and opening-hour caveats.

### Differentiation worth paying for

- Claim-level historical sourcing and explicit uncertainty rather than an open-publishing catalogue.
- Date-aware alternatives when a location closes or a major Leiden event changes the route.
- Location-specific “look around” prompts and practical access guidance.
- Coherent routes built around Leiden rather than generic articles pinned to a global map.
- Sensitive-history standards and no fabricated reconstructions.

### Claims to avoid

- Do not claim the largest catalogue, the cheapest tour or the highest rating.
- Do not compare star ratings without storefront, country, date and review count.
- Do not describe free museum guides as equivalent standalone businesses; admission and public or
  philanthropic funding change their economics.
- Do not call izi.TRAVEL entirely free. Its creator CMS can be free while the current consumer app
  still contains advertising, subscriptions and paid tours.

## Limitations and next validation

- Store prices are regional and can change without notice. Recheck them when the business case is
  approved and again before any public comparison.
- Public pricing does not reveal conversion, refunds, customer-acquisition cost or net revenue.
- App stars measure the whole app across many destinations, not the quality of one city.
- The proposed Leiden prices require five to eight moderated purchase tests and a small live landing
  page test. Ask participants to choose between the free preview, a single route, the full city and
  a human guide; record the reason before discussing price.
