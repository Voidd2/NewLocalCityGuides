## 0. READ THIS BEFORE USING THIS FILE

This is a **discovery** document, not a research document.

Its purpose is to make sure that when Leiden's first 8–15 production locations are chosen
(`LDN-MVP-001`), they are chosen from the whole city and the whole of its history — not
from the five places a tourist office would name first.

Accordingly:

1. **Nothing here is verified history yet.** Every entry carries a `Confidence` field.
   Only `HIGH` entries were cross-checked against an institutional or academic source
   during this pass. `MEDIUM` and `LOW` entries are *leads*.
2. **No entry may be used as the basis for visitor-facing copy or a video script** until it
   has passed `R01` (deep research) and `R02` (source verification). Master §3.
3. **Coordinates are deliberately absent.** Guessing a lat/long is inventing data. Every
   entry gives a street-level locator instead; `ENG-GEO-001` resolves them from PDOK/BAG.
4. **Story/video strength numbers are estimates**, written to help prioritise the scoring
   pass. They are *not* the 15-criteria scores from master §5 Phase 1B. That is `LDN-SCORE-001`.
5. **No tier has been assigned.** Tiering happens after scoring.

### LDN-C001 — De Burcht van Leiden
- **Type:** Motte / shell keep, now a public park
- **Locator:** Burgsteeg, on the mound between Oude Rijn and Nieuwe Rijn · **Coordinates:** TBD
- **Era(s):** late 9th–10th c. (earliest excavated timber) · c. 1150 (mound at full height) · 2nd half 13th c. (stone ring wall) · 19th–20th c. (public park)
- **Theme:** Why Leiden exists at all; comital power; water geography
- **Event/person/process:** Artificial mound raised in phases over sods and clay; the circular stone wall possibly built under Floris V; from 1206 firmly in the hands of the Count of Holland. Designated an archaeological national monument.
- **What physically happened here:** A defensible high point was deliberately built at the fork of the Rhine, controlling river traffic. Everything Leiden became grew outward from this decision.
- **Visible today:** YES — mound, ring wall and views intact
- **Accessibility:** PARTIAL — the mound is reached by steps; the base is level
- **Source leads:** Erfgoed Leiden en Omstreken (archaeology + heritage register); RCE archaeological monument designation; *Holland Historisch Tijdschrift* article on the Burcht; Leiden municipality
- **Story (est.):** 5 · **Video (est.):** 5
- **Route value:** Extremely high — natural opening stop for any route; physically and narratively the city's origin point
- **Overlap:** C002 (the confluence it guards), C009 (Vismarkt below it)
- **Confidence:** `HIGH`
- **Notes:** Strongest "look around you" potential in the city — from the wall you can see the two Rhine branches, the church towers, and the medieval street pattern at once. Type A (present→past) video is obvious here.

### LDN-C045 — Steenschuur and the gunpowder disaster of 1807
- **Type:** Canal / disaster site
- **Locator:** Steenschuur, in the extension of the Rapenburg · **Coordinates:** TBD
- **Era(s):** **12 January 1807**
- **Theme:** A city centre erased in one second
- **Event/person/process:** At about a quarter past four in the afternoon of 12 January 1807, a vessel carrying some **37,000 Dutch pounds of gunpowder**, travelling from Haarlem to Delft, exploded at the **Steenschuur**. **151 people were killed**, over **2,000 injured**, and roughly **220 houses** destroyed or made uninhabitable; windows broke and roof tiles came off across the city. - **What physically happened here:** A residential quarter in the middle of a university city ceased to exist between one moment and the next.
- **Visible today:** PARTIAL — the void is legible in the city plan (see C014); rebuilt frontages date from after 1807
- **Accessibility:** YES
- **Source leads:** Erfgoed Leiden (*Vondst van de week*: the disaster and the roof of Rapenburg 112); Rijksmuseum prints — *Ruïnes op het Rapenburg te Leiden na de ramp, 1807*, *Buskruitramp te Leiden, 1807*, *Het Rapenburg te Leiden, 1807*; municipal archives; Historiek

- **Confidence:** `HIGH`

## Known gaps — what this pass did NOT find

State these plainly rather than pretending the register is complete.

1. **Roman Leiden.** The Rhine mouth region has significant Roman-period archaeology, but
   nothing in the city centre was established as a defensible visitor location in this pass.
   Needs an archaeology-specific search (RMO, RCE, Erfgoed Leiden excavation reports).
2. **The 1944 bombing of Leiden.** An Allied bombing of Leiden in late 1944 is part of the
   city's WWII memory, but neither the date, the target, the area hit nor the casualty
   figures could be confirmed in this pass. **Deliberately excluded rather than guessed.**
   High priority for `LDN-VERIFY-001`.
3. **Post-war and modern Leiden.** Reconstruction, 1960s–70s urban renewal, student
   housing, the Bio Science Park, migration since 1960. Currently near-absent, and the
   register is weaker for it.
4. **Women's history.** Present only implicitly (textile labour, hofjes, the plague-dead
   daughter of C041). No candidate is *about* a documented Leiden woman. This is a real
   gap and it should be closed before MVP selection, not after.
5. **Guilds, hospitals, orphanages.** Named in master §5 as targets; only partially reached
   (C022, C028, C040). The Heilige Geest weeshuis and the guild system deserve their own pass.
6. **Justice and public punishment.** Only C005. The city's judicial archives almost
   certainly support more.
7. **Coordinates.** None. By design — `ENG-GEO-001`.
