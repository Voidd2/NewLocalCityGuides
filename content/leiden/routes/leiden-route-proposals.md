## 0. THE RULE THIS FOLLOWS

Master §17: *"Routes are stories, not only stop lists."*

So each route below has an **arc**, not just an order. Where the research turned up a real
connection between two locations, the route uses it — because those connections were found, not
invented, and they are what makes a route feel like something rather than a list.

**Master §17 also requires both free exploration and curated routes, with neither secondary.**
These proposals are for the curated side. Every location must also work as a standalone stop
for someone who just walked past it.

## 2. ROUTE PROPOSALS

### RT001 — "Where the city started" · ~45 min · 4 stops
**Arc:** why anything is here at all.
`L001 Burcht` → `L002 Vismarkt` → `L003 Koornbrug` → `L004 Blauwe Steen`

Two rivers meet; someone builds a hill; a market grows under it; a bridge becomes a warehouse;
and the exact centre of the resulting city is a stone in the road that nobody looks at.

**Why it works:** the shortest, cheapest, most accessible route. All four stops are free,
outdoors and always open. **No ticket, no opening hours, no seasonality.** The natural default
for a first-time visitor with an hour — and the only route that works at 9pm in November.
**Uses the L001→L002 water connection.**

---

### RT002 — "Judged here, killed there" · ~40 min · 3 stops
**Arc:** the machinery of justice, followed on foot.
`L004 Blauwe Steen` → *(the Diefsteeg)* → `L005 Gravensteen` → `L006 Pieterskerk`

Stand where sentence was passed; walk the lane the condemned were taken down; arrive at the
building where the judges had a gallery built so they could watch. The Pieterskerkhof, where the
last execution took place in 1856, is the churchyard of the third stop.

**Why it works:** the walk *is* the narrative. Nothing else in the MVP does that.
⚠️ **Tone constraint (from `L004-R01` §7 and `L005-R01` §7):** tell it plainly. No dramatised
executioner. And the 1856 case carries an editorial ruling still pending from the user.
**Not suitable as the family route.**

---

### RT003 — "People who came here" · ~1 hr · 4 stops
**Arc:** Leiden as a city built by arrivals.
`L006 Pieterskerk` → `L007 Pilgrims' quarter` → `L008 Jean Pesijnhofje` → `L013 Wevershuis`

Robinson's grave; the ground where his people built 21 houses; the hofje a Walloon widow founded
on that same ground in 1683; and the trade nearly all of them actually worked in — at the scale
of one house, with every resident since 1561 named on the wall.

**Why it works:** it is the register's strongest through-line, and every link in it is
documented. It also carries the MVP's named woman (**Marie de Lannoy**) and its labour and
women's-work content.
⚠️ **Access-constrained:** L008 is people's homes and L013 has low accessibility and limited
hours. This route needs the most careful practical information of any.

---

### RT004 — "What they found out here" · ~1 hr · 3 stops
**Arc:** a small city that changed what Europe knew.
`L010 Hortus Botanicus` → `L011 Weddesteeg` → `L009 Buskruitramp 1807`

A teaching garden 35 by 40 metres holding a thousand plants, whose tulips were stolen; a
miller's son who became the most famous painter in the country; and the afternoon in 1807 when
a gunpowder barge took out a quarter of the city and changed national law.

**Why it works:** three completely different kinds of story, all along the Rapenburg spine.
**The Rapenburg itself is the narration between stops** — it is a route, not a stop.
⚠️ L010 is ticketed and **seasonal**.

---

### RT005 — "Leiden Essentials" · ~2 hrs · 8 stops
**Arc:** origin → power → belief → catastrophe → knowledge.
`L001` → `L002` → `L004` → `L005` → `L006` → `L007` → `L009` → `L010`

The introduction route. Matches the wireframe's "Leiden Essentials · 1,5 uur · 8 locaties"
framing (screen 5.1), though the honest estimate is closer to **two hours**.

⚠️ **This route does not include L012, L013 or L014** — the three reserved slots. That is a
real problem: an "essentials" route that omits the Siege, the textile industry and the war
reproduces exactly the bias `LDN-COVERAGE-001` corrected. **See §4.**

---

### RT006 — "One building, four centuries" · ~30 min · 1 site
**Arc:** the Academy Building alone.
`L010 Hortus` + `L014 Cleveringa` + candidates `C017`, `C018`, `C061`

The university given as the reward for a siege; a garden built for medical teaching; a room
where doctoral candidates have signed the wall for centuries; the lecture hall where a professor
objected in 1940; and the 1873 refusal and 1878 arrival of the first women students.

**Why it works:** it is a route you barely walk. Good for bad weather, limited mobility, or a
visitor with half an hour.
⚠️ Depends entirely on **access** to the Academy Building, which is unresolved for L014.
⚠️ **DEC-016 applies in full** to the L014 segment.

## 4. ⚠️ THE PROBLEM WITH "ESSENTIALS" — a decision for the user

RT005 is the route most visitors will pick, and as drafted it drops all three reserved slots.
The coverage analysis established that ranking alone produces an MVP without the Siege or the
war. **A default route chosen by convenience reintroduces the same failure at the route layer.**

Three options:

| Option | Effect |
|---|---|
| **A.** Leave RT005 as the efficient eight | Most walkable. Silently drops the Siege, textiles and WWII from what most visitors see |
| **B.** Swap two stops for **L012** and **L013** | Restores coverage. Costs walking time — L013 is east, away from the spine |
| **C.** ⭐ **Make RT005 nine stops by adding L012** | L012 is anchored at De Waag, **which sits between L002 and L004** — almost no detour. It restores the Siege for a few minutes' walking |

**Recommendation: C.** It is nearly free, and an "essentials" route for Leiden that never
mentions Leidens Ontzet would be a strange thing to ship.

**Flagged, not decided.**
