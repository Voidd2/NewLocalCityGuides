## 1. THE SHORT ANSWER

A weaver's house from about 1560 that survived because its last occupant never repaired
anything — and a wall inside listing everyone who has lived there since 1561.

---

---

## 3. WHY IT SURVIVED — the accidental preservation

**The last inhabitant made no significant repairs or alterations in forty years.** The house had
**no shower, no sink, and not even a kitchen**. That is why the fire brigade condemned it — and
it is exactly why it survives in near-original condition.

Every other weaver's house in Leiden was modernised, and is therefore gone as evidence. This one
was not, because somebody lived in it without changing it. **The building survives as a
by-product of poverty, or of stubbornness, or both** — and `R02` should try to find out which,
because that person's name and circumstances would make this location.

---

## 4. ⭐ THE WALL

> **In the hallway is a wall painting with the names of the weavers and residents, beginning in
> 1561.**

This is the best physical object found anywhere in this project.

- It is a **verified, visible, specific thing to point at** — master §12's exact requirement.
- It answers master §9's *"are specific historical people documented?"* with a list of names
  on a wall, in the room the visitor is standing in.
- It converts an abstraction — "Leiden's textile industry employed tens of thousands" — into
  **individual people who lived in this house in sequence for over 450 years**.
- It is the **antidote** to L013's stated purpose failing. The reserved slot exists to carry
  the labour story (candidate C057: the spinsters, the ~40% of textile workers under sixteen
  around 1860). An abstract statistic becomes bearable and memorable next to a list of names.

⚠️ `R02` must establish: how many names, what period they cover, whether the painting is
historic or a modern presentation, and whether it can be photographed.

## 5. PEOPLE AND ACTION

- **Cornelis Janszoon**, linen weaver, first known inhabitant, 1561.
- Everyone else on the wall.
- **Home weavers** supplying the **drapers' guild** — piecework, at home, to a guild standard
  (→ candidate C064, the guild house).
- **The Pilgrims** (→ L007): most worked in this trade, poorly paid. The Wevershuis itself
  publishes on *Pilgrims in de Leidse textielwereld, 1609–1620*.
- **Women and children** (candidate C057) — the ones the register says are least visible.

**What physically happened here:** a family lived, slept, ate and worked in the same small
space as the loom that fed them. The house *is* the working condition.

---

## 6. NARRATIVE

**Candidate hook:** *"Everyone who has lived here since 1561 is written on the wall."*

- **Conflict:** piecework, poverty, and a guild standard to meet.
- **Transformation:** a workplace → a condemned house → a museum.
- **Unexpected:** it survives **because** nobody could afford to improve it.
- **Human perspective:** it is a list of names in a hallway.
