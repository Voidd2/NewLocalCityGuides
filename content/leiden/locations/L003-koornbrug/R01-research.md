## 3. THE PLACE (master §9)

A fixed stone arch bridge with a **double canopy** over the Nieuwe Rijn.

- **1642** — three arches, dressed natural stone fronts.
- **1824** — the two **neoclassical** roofs, to keep the goods dry.
- Designed by **Salomon van der Paauw (1794–1869)**, Leiden's city architect from
  **17 August 1816 to 31 December 1862** — a surveyor, cartographer, water engineer, architect
  and garden architect.
- **Rijksmonument since 1968.** Roofs substantially restored in **1978**.

### How the structure works — the reason it looks like that
- **Grain was stored under the roof.**
- **The holes in the ceiling are ventilation** — grain in bulk must breathe or it spoils.
- The roofing carries **Leiden's coat of arms — the crossed keys — surrounded by ears of grain.**

The building explains its own function to anyone who looks up. That is rare and it is the
whole case for this stop.

## 4. PEOPLE AND ACTION

- **Salomon van der Paauw** — city architect for 46 years. He is a thread running through
  19th-century Leiden and worth following: one man shaped a great deal of what visitors see.
- **Grain merchants** — trading on a bridge, under cover, above moving water.

**What physically happened:** grain arrived by boat on the Nieuwe Rijn, was hauled up, traded
on the bridge deck, stored under the canopy, and moved on. The bridge was simultaneously a
crossing, a marketplace and a warehouse.

⚠️ `R04` gap: the **daily working routine** — who hauled, how it was measured, how disputes were
settled — is not established. Municipal grain-trade ordinances will cover it (`R02`).

## 9. R05 — UNCERTAINTY REGISTER

### VERIFIED
- Name from **1443**, when the grain market was first held here.
- Present bridge **1642** — three arches, dressed stone fronts.
- **Roofs 1824**, neoclassical, by **Salomon van der Paauw**, city architect 1816–1862.
- Grain stored under the roof; **ceiling holes are ventilation**.
- Roofing carries **Leiden's keys surrounded by ears of grain**.
- **Rijksmonument since 1968**; roofs restored **1978**.
- Restoration of the canopy pillar foundations began **3 November 2025**, scheduled to
  **24 April 2026**, due to subsidence.

  gives **1825**; the bicentenary was marked in 2024–25. **Establish which year is correct, or
