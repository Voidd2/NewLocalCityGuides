## 1. THE SHORT ANSWER

A garden 35 by 40 metres, planted in a single season in 1594, holding over a thousand kinds of
plant — and people climbed the fence to steal the tulips.

---

## 2. THE PLACE (master §9)

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **9 February 1590** | The university receives permission from the municipality — **the founding date** |
| **1590** | It is given the *"Plaetse achter de Universiteyt"* — the ground behind the former convent of the **Witte Nonnen**, now the Academy building |
| **1592** | **Carolus Clusius (1526–1609)** agrees to come to Leiden |
| **End of 1593** | Clusius arrives |
| **1594** | Planting begins, with the apothecary **Dirck Outgaertsz. Cluyt** — **completed in one season** |
| **1709–1730** | Under **Herman Boerhaave**, the collection grows to nearly **6,000** species |
| **2008 onward** | The **Clusiustuin** — a reconstruction of the garden as it was around 1600, designed from the original 1594 plan |

**The original garden was about 35 × 40 metres** and held **more than 1,000 different plants**.
The **first planting list ran to 1,585 plants** — made possible by Clusius's international
correspondence and Cluyt's own collection.

Purpose: it was built for **medical education**. This was not decorative. It was teaching
equipment.

## 3. PEOPLE

- **Carolus Clusius** — first **praefectus**. A botanist with a Europe-wide correspondence
  network, which is *how* a small garden got 1,585 plants in one season.
- **Dirck Outgaertsz. Cluyt** — apothecary, Clusius's right hand, who supplied plants and
  organised the practicalities. **He should not be a footnote**: the garden exists because a
  scholar's network met a working apothecary's collection.
- **Herman Boerhaave** — also buried in the **Pieterskerk** (→ L006).

## 4. THE ACTION — and the theft

Clusius was the first in the Netherlands to successfully cultivate **tulips**.

**He put a small fence around his tulip bulbs. It was not enough — tulips were stolen from his
hortus.**

That is the detail this location turns on. The Dutch tulip trade, one of the most famous
economic stories in European history, has among its origins a professor's fence failing to keep
people out of a university garden.

⚠️ **One claim must not be used.** A source says Clusius received his first tulip bulbs
**"from the Ottoman Sultan Suleyman"**. Suleiman I died in **1566**; Clusius arrived in Leiden
in **1593**. As stated it is chronologically impossible. The Ottoman route is real, but the
attribution is garbled — `R02` must establish the actual provenance or the claim is dropped.

## 5. NARRATIVE

**Candidate hook:** *"He built a fence around the tulips. It didn't work."*

- **Conflict:** a scholar versus thieves, and knowledge versus profit.
- **Transformation:** a teaching plot → a global trade → a national symbol.
- **Scale:** 35 × 40 metres. A visitor can see the whole original footprint at once.
- **Unexpected:** the first planting list has **1,585 entries** and was achieved **in one season**.
