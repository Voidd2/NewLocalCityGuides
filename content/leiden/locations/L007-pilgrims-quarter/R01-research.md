## 1. THE SHORT ANSWER

Before they were the Pilgrim Fathers they were badly paid textile workers in a Leiden back
alley, for eleven years, and most of them never left.

---

## 2. THE PLACE (master §9)

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **1609** | English religious refugees arrive, led by **William Bradford** and **John Robinson**. About **300** Pilgrims live in various parts of Leiden |
| **Shortly after 1611** | Robinson buys a house with ground near the Pieterskerk; members of his group build **21 small houses**. The area becomes known as the **Engelse Poort** — the English Gate |
| **1620** | They begin to leave |
| **1625** | Robinson, who stayed, is buried in the **Pieterskerk** (→ **L006**) |
| **1683** | The houses give way to the **Jean Pesijnhof**, Kloksteeg 21 (→ **L008**) |

**L007 and L008 are the same ground.** The English Gate became the hofje. One refugee
community's houses were bought up and replaced by an almshouse founded by another refugee's
widow. That is not a coincidence to be glossed over — it is the location's spine.

---

## 3. WHAT THEY ACTUALLY DID HERE (master §9 — Action)

This is where the popular story is usually wrong, and where the research is unambiguous.

- **Most Pilgrims worked in Leiden as labourers in the textile industry and were poorly paid.**
- Many worked as **weavers**.
- Others were **carpenters, tailors and printers** — also poorly paid work.
- They lived in **tiny houses with large families**.

They were not passing through on the way to America. They were **economic and religious
migrants doing hard, badly paid work in the trade that ran this city** — the same trade as
**L013**, the Wevershuis, and the same trade as the Southern Netherlandish refugees who came a
generation earlier (candidate C037).

**They left in 1620 partly because they feared losing their identity** — not because Leiden
failed them.

⚠️ One source describes Bradford as a member of a cloth guild meeting in "St. Louis Cathedral".
That is almost certainly garbled and there is no such building in Leiden. **Do not use it.**
`R02` should establish whether Bradford was in a Leiden guild at all — if so, it links to
candidate C064.

## 4. NARRATIVE

**Candidate hook:** *"They lived here eleven years. Most of them never left."*

- **Conflict:** faith versus assimilation. They left because their children were becoming Dutch.
- **Transformation:** obscure textile labourers → founding myth of a nation.
- **Unexpected:** of ~300, only a minority sailed. Leiden is not a footnote in their story;
  for most of them it is the whole story.
- **Human perspective:** large families in tiny houses, paid badly, for eleven years.

**Editorial requirement (master §3, honesty):** the colonial aftermath must not be omitted or
sentimentalised. The people who left founded a colony on land that was not empty. This location
does not need to tell that whole story, but it must not tell a version that pretends it did
not happen.

## 7. R05 — UNCERTAINTY REGISTER

### VERIFIED
- Arrival **1609**; leaders **William Bradford** and **John Robinson**; about **300** Pilgrims.
- Robinson bought a house with ground near the Pieterskerk; **21 small houses** were built;
  the area was called the **Engelse Poort**.
- The gate was built **shortly after 1611** by members of Robinson's English group.
- Departures begin **1620**; Robinson stayed and was **buried in the Pieterskerk in 1625**.
- **Most worked in the textile industry and were poorly paid**; also carpenters, tailors,
  printers; they lived in tiny houses with large families.
- **1683** — the site becomes the Jean Pesijnhof.

### CONFLICTING — `R02` MUST RESOLVE
- Whether the Pilgrim houses were **demolished to make way for** the hofje, or whether Marie de
  Lannoy **bought and reused** some of them. Sources say both. This matters: if any fabric
  survives, L008 contains physical remains of L007.

### DO NOT USE
- The "cloth guild in St. Louis Cathedral" claim. Garbled.

### NOT ESTABLISHED
- What the 21 houses looked like (blocking for video).
- How many of the ~300 actually sailed. **This is the number the whole story turns on** and it
  should be nailed down precisely.
