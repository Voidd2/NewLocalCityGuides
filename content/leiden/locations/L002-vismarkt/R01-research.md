## 1. THE SHORT ANSWER

A place that smelled so bad, for so long, that the city built a fountain and ran a pipe
**under the river from a castle** to fix it.

---

## 2. THE PLACE (master §9)

The Vismarkt lies between the Stadhuisplein and the Nieuwe Rijn, just before the Nieuwe Rijn
meets the Oude Rijn — i.e. directly below **L001**.

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **12th century** | First bridge on the **Visbrug** site — described as Leiden's oldest bridge |
| Medieval | Fish traded here by merchants from **Katwijk and Noordwijk**, which is where the bridge's name comes from |
| **1518** | The fish market moves from the town hall (the "old Vismarkt") to the **Havendijk** |
| **1693** | The **Visfontein** is built |
| **1906** | The municipality moves the fish market to the **Vollersgracht**; the stalls go with it, and a steel cover is added later |
| Today | A general market still runs here — **Wednesdays and Saturdays** |

The **vishal**'s two outer spaces held the **visbanken** (fish stalls).

---

## 3. THE STORY THIS LOCATION IS ACTUALLY ABOUT — the Visfontein, 1693

This is the find of the `R01` pass and it changes the location.

**The problem:** fishmongers kept live fish in baskets **in the canal**. In summer the canal
water was not clean enough, and there were **complaints**.

**The city's answer, in 1693:** build a fountain supplying clean water, and feed it from the
Burcht.

**How it worked:**
- Water was collected **at the foot of the castle hill**.
- A **horse mill** pumped it through **lead pipes, 150 metres long**, up to a large **water
  cellar on the Burcht**.
- A **ten-metre height difference** gave the fountain its pressure.
- From the cellar the water ran **under the Nieuwe Rijn** — and **through a house** — to the
  Vismarkt.
- Archaeologists have **found the connection** between the reservoirs in the castle hill and
  the Visfontein.

The fountain was **designed and made by the sculptor Jacob Roman in 1693**.

**Today** it is simply connected to the mains. **In winter the sculpture is wrapped** to
protect it from the weather — see `PracticalInfo` below; this is a real visitor-facing fact.

### Why this matters for the route
**L001 and L002 are physically joined by a 17th-century pipe running under the river.** Two
consecutive stops, one continuous story: the fortress became the city's water tower, and what
it watered was the fish market. Master §17 asks routes to be stories rather than stop lists —
this is the connective tissue, already built.

## 4. PEOPLE AND ACTION (master §9)

- **Fish merchants from Katwijk and Noordwijk** — the trade was not local; it came from the coast.
- **Jacob Roman** — sculptor, designed and made the Visfontein, 1693.
- **The city government** — acted on complaints from its own market traders.
- **The fish sellers** — who kept fish alive in baskets in the canal, and complained.

**What physically happened:** fish arrived by water, were kept alive in canal baskets, killed,
gutted and sold at the visbanken. The waste went somewhere. The smell, the noise, the flies and
the water were the defining sensory facts of this spot for centuries — and are now entirely gone.

⚠️ `R04` gap: **what happened to the waste** is not established for Leiden. A comparison source
for Amsterdam describes fishwives being required to scrub the benches daily after market and
waste being barrowed to the city refuse barge. **That is Amsterdam, not Leiden.** Do not
transplant it. Leiden's own market ordinances (*keuren*) will say — that is an `R02` task.

## 5. NARRATIVE

**Candidate hook:** *"They built a fountain because this place stank."*

- **Conflict:** traders versus the water they had to use.
- **Transformation:** from the loudest, smelliest working corner of Leiden to a pleasant square.
- **Unexpected detail:** the fix involved a **horse walking in circles** to pump water **uphill
  into a castle** so it could come **back down under a river**.
- **Human perspective:** someone complained, and the city listened, and the answer is still
  standing in the square.

## 8. R05 — UNCERTAINTY REGISTER

### VERIFIED
- The **Visfontein dates from 1693** and was designed and made by **Jacob Roman**.
- It was built because the **canal water was not clean enough** in summer for fish kept in
  baskets, and the fish sellers complained.
- The water came from a **cellar under the Burcht**, pumped by a **horse mill** through
  **150 m of lead pipe**, with a **ten-metre height difference** driving the fountain, running
  **under the Nieuwe Rijn** to the Vismarkt.
- Archaeologists have **confirmed the connection** between the Burcht reservoirs and the fountain.
- **1518** — the market moved from the town hall to the Havendijk.
- **1906** — the fish market moved to the Vollersgracht.
- The market still runs **Wednesdays and Saturdays**.
- The fountain is now on the mains, and **the sculpture is wrapped in winter**.

### PROBABLE — confirm in `R02`
- The bridge name derives from fish merchants from **Katwijk and Noordwijk**.
- A first bridge on the Visbrug site in the **12th century**.
- The vishal's two outer bays held the visbanken.

### ⚠️ SUPERLATIVES — DEC-013 APPLIES, DO NOT PUBLISH UNVERIFIED
- **"Leiden's oldest bridge"** (Visbrug).
- **"The only fixed river crossing over the Rhine between Utrecht and the North Sea"** (with
  the Sint Jansbrug) at that date.
- **"Nearly 900 years"** of weekly market on this spot.

All three are striking, all three are exactly the kind of claim the verification pass found to
be wrong elsewhere (the "oldest hofje" was out by 25 years). Each needs an institutional source
or it does not appear.

### NOT ESTABLISHED — do not fill from elsewhere
- Leiden's fish-waste disposal and market hygiene rules. The Amsterdam parallel is **not
  evidence for Leiden**.
