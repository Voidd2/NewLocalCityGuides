## 1. THE SHORT ANSWER

The exact middle of medieval Leiden, and the exact spot where people were told they were going
to die. Thousands of people walk over it every day.

---

## 2. ⚠️ TWO CORRECTIONS TO THE CANDIDATE ENTRY

`R01` changed two things that the discovery pass got wrong or half-right. Both matter.

### Correction 1 — the stone you look at is from 1910
The current stone is **not the original**. A new stone was placed in **1910** through the
efforts of the **Oud Leiden** association, **at the original location**.

The product must therefore never say *"you are standing on the stone where sentence was
passed."* The honest and still-remarkable version is: **you are standing on the spot.** The
stone is a replacement marking a place the city decided was worth keeping marked — which is
itself a story about memory.

### Correction 2 — there are three stones, and the colour theory is weaker than it looked
The candidate entry recorded a reading in which blue meant civil authority, red ecclesiastical
and white feudal. The **more commonly given interpretation** is different and more concrete:
the stones marked **different areas of jurisdiction**.

| Stone | Jurisdiction |
|---|---|
| **Blauwe Steen** | Residents of the **southern bank of the Rhine** |
| **Rode Steen** | The **schout's district of Marendorp** |
| **Witte Steen** | The area **around the Burcht** (→ L001) |

All three are now officially designated as a monument. **Use the jurisdiction reading; treat
the colour-symbolism reading as a secondary theory if it is used at all.**

## 3. THE PLACE (master §9)

A smooth **hexagonal blue slate stone**, from Belgium, set into the roadway at the junction of
the **Breestraat**, the **Pieterskerkchoorsteeg** and the **Maarsmansteeg**.

- It marks the symbolic centre of the **four vierendelen** — the quarters into which medieval
  Leiden was divided **until its first expansion in 1294**.
- **1321** — first mentioned. Notably, **not primarily as a boundary marker but as a place of
  justice.**
- From the 14th century it functioned in the administration of justice.
- **1463** — the carrying out of death sentences moves to the square before the Gravensteen (L005).
- **1910** — the present stone is laid.

---

## 4. THE ACTION — and the walk that follows it

This is the discovery that changes the route.

1. The accused **stood at the stone** to hear sentence.
2. The sentence was **called out from the Roepstoel** — a raised platform on the **town hall
   façade**, with steps leading up to a **blank wall**.
3. The condemned were then taken **through the Diefsteeg — "Thieves' Lane" — to 't Gerecht**,
   where the Leiden executioner carried out the sentence.

**The walk from L004 to L005 is the condemned's walk.** It is short, it is still there, and it
still has the name. Master §17 asks that routes be stories rather than stop lists: here the
*street between two stops* is the story, and the visitor performs it with their feet.

**This is the strongest route mechanic found anywhere in the project so far.** It should be
handled with care rather than relish — see §7.

## 5. NARRATIVE

**Candidate hook:** *"Look down. You are standing in the middle of the city."*

- **Unexpected detail:** the centre of Leiden is a stone in a road that nobody looks at.
- **Conflict:** three stones, three jurisdictions, one city arguing with itself about who
  answered to whom.
- **Transformation:** a place of judgement became a place of traffic.
- **The blank wall:** the Roepstoel's steps lead up to nothing. The reason why is an `R02`
  question and potentially a very good one.

## 7. ⚠️ EDITORIAL NOTE — TONE

This location and L005 are about people being killed by the state. The material is genuinely
gripping, which is exactly the risk: master §3 puts accuracy above spectacle, and master §20
warns against making the city into a themed attraction.

**Recommendation:** tell it plainly and let it be grim on its own. No dramatised executioner,
no "gruesome" framing, no invented crowd reactions. The documented facts — a stone, a shouted
sentence, a lane with a name, a short walk — are stronger than any dramatisation.

## 9. R05 — UNCERTAINTY REGISTER

### VERIFIED
- Hexagonal **blue slate** stone, Belgian, set in the roadway at Breestraat /
  Pieterskerkchoorsteeg / Maarsmansteeg.
- Marks the centre of the **four vierendelen**, the division that stood **until the first
  expansion of 1294**.
- **First mentioned in 1321**, as a place of justice rather than a boundary marker.
- Sentences were proclaimed from the **Roepstoel** on the town hall façade.
- The condemned were taken through the **Diefsteeg** to **'t Gerecht**.
- **The present stone dates from 1910**, laid by Oud Leiden at the original location.
- Three stones — **Blauwe, Rode, Witte** — marking different jurisdictions; all now monuments.

### PROBABLE — confirm in `R02`
- The specific jurisdictions of each stone as listed in §2.
- That suspects literally **stood on** the stone (sources say "believed to").

### SECONDARY THEORY — do not lead with it
- Blue = civil, red = ecclesiastical, white = feudal authority.

### NOT ESTABLISHED
- Why the Roepstoel's steps lead to a **blank wall**. Potentially an excellent detail.
- Whether any **named, documented sentencing** at this stone survives in the court records.
  If one does, it would transform this location (master §9: "are specific historical people
  documented?").
