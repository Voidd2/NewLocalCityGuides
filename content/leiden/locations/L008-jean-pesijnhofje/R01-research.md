## 1. ⚠️ THE CORRECTION THAT CHANGES THIS LOCATION

The candidate entry (`C041`) said Jean Pesijn founded the hofje after his daughter died of
plague. **The better sources say something different and more significant.**

> **The hofje was founded in 1683 by Marie de Lannoy — Jean Pesijn's widow — after his death.**

She came from **Lille**, and arranged **in her testament** that elderly members of the
**Walloon Reformed Church** could live there. After her husband's death she **bought small
houses in the English Gate on the Kloksteeg** — the Pilgrims' plot (→ **L007**).

**This matters for three reasons:**

1. **It is factually different.** Jean Pesijn was dead. Writing "Jean Pesijn built this" would
   be wrong, and the hofje carries his name precisely because a woman named it after him.
2. **It gives the MVP a named woman with agency.** The coverage analysis recorded as a real
   failing that no woman in the fourteen selected locations had a name. **Marie de Lannoy now
   does** — a refugee from Lille who bought property and wrote a will that is still being
   executed 340 years later. That is not a footnote; it is the location.
3. **The plague-daughter story now needs re-verification.** It may still be true as motivation,
   but it attached to the wrong person. `R02` must check it before it is ever published.

## 2. THE PLACE (master §9)

- **Kloksteeg 21.** Twelve almshouses, managed by the **Jean Pesijnhof** foundation.
- Closed off by a **gate building of 1683** with a **central chimney** and a **stone entrance
  gate**, above which is the **regents' chamber**.
- **Still managed by regents** — reportedly the only Leiden hofje of which that is true.
- Built on the site of the **Engelse Poort**, the Pilgrims' quarter (→ L007), which was built
  shortly after 1611.

## 3. PEOPLE

- **Marie de Lannoy** — founder, widow of Jean Pesijn, from Lille. The central figure.
- **Jean Pesijn** — refugee from near Lille; the hofje bears his name.
- **The Walloon Reformed Church** — the intended community: French-speaking Protestant refugees.
- **The regents** — a governing body that has run this place continuously since 1683.
- **The residents** — then and now.

## 4. THE LAYERS — why this small courtyard is worth a stop

Three refugee communities on one plot of ground, in sequence:

1. **c. 1611–1683** — English religious refugees (the Pilgrims) build 21 houses here.
2. **1683** — a **Walloon** refugee's widow buys the site and founds an almshouse for elderly
   **French-speaking Protestant refugees**.
3. **Today** — still housing people, still run by regents, under a will written in 1683.

Leiden's through-line — a city repeatedly rebuilt by people who arrived from somewhere else —
is compressed into about thirty square metres, and it is still running.

## 5. NARRATIVE

**Candidate hook:** *"A widow bought this ground and wrote a will. It is still being obeyed."*

- **Human perspective:** a woman, a refugee, acting alone after her husband's death.
- **Transformation:** English houses → Walloon almshouse → still housing people.
- **Continuity:** the 1683 instruction has never stopped being carried out. Almost nothing else
  in this app is still doing what it was set up to do.

## 6. ⚠️ ACCESS — A HARD CONSTRAINT, NOT A NOTE

**People live here.** This is not a museum.

- Any *Look Around You* instruction must respect residents' privacy and the hofje's own rules.
- The product must **not** route large numbers of visitors through a private courtyard.
- `R02` must establish the foundation's actual position on visitors, and the app must state it
  accurately — including "you may look from the gate but not enter", if that is the answer.
- **If the foundation does not want visitors, that answer is final** and the location becomes a
  gate-only stop. That would still work: the 1683 gate, the chimney and the regents' chamber
  above it are all visible from the Kloksteeg.

This is the clearest case in the MVP where master §12's rule — only point at what is genuinely
available — has an ethical as well as a factual edge.
