The siege ended, the relief fleet handed out herring and white bread, and Leiden has been
re-enacting the meal ever since — though not for as long as people think.

---

## 2. WHAT HAPPENED, 3 OCTOBER 1574

- The Spanish siege ended on **3 October 1574**.
- The **Watergeuzen** came in **via the Vliet** and distributed **herring and white bread** to
  the starving population.
- In the **abandoned Spanish camp** the liberators found **carrots, onions and parsnips** — from
  which **hutspot** was made.
- Leiden has commemorated the day ever since; the **3 Octoberfeest** is the result.

## 4. ⚠️ THE TRADITION IS 19th-CENTURY, NOT 16th

**"Since 1886"** is the single most important correction in this file.

The **event** is 1574. The **distribution as practised** dates from **1886**. The product must
not say or imply that Leiden has been handing out herring and white bread continuously for 450
years, because the sources do not say that.

The honest version is more interesting: a city took a 300-year-old memory and, in 1886,
deliberately built a ritual to keep it. That is a story about **how memory is made**, which
suits master §1's "stand where history happened" better than a false claim of unbroken custom.

---

## 5. ⚠️ TWO MORE THINGS TO GET RIGHT

**The length of the siege.** One source says the siege ran from **October 1573 to 3 October
1574**; another says it lasted **"ruim vier maanden"** — just over four months. Both can be
true: the siege had **two phases**, lifted in March 1574 and resumed in May. `R02` must
establish the phases and the product must not give a single misleading duration.

**Hutspot.** The story that the liberators found the ingredients in the Spanish camp is
**tradition**. Master §11 forbids presenting unsourced legend as fact. Establish what the
contemporary sources actually support; if the answer is "this is what Leiden has told itself",
say exactly that — it is not a weaker story.
