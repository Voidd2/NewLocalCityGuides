## 1. THE SHORT ANSWER

A building where the counts of Holland lived, that became a prison, that acquired a gallery so
the judges could watch the executions they had ordered.

---

## 2. THE PLACE (master §9)

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **Early 13th c.** | Built. In the High Middle Ages the counts of Holland held a court here — a **residence, a chapel** (which became the **Pieterskerk**, → L006) **and an orchard** |
| Probably 13th c. | A **tower**, functioning as the **comital prison** |
| **15th c.** | **Donated to the city of Leiden**, which expanded it over the following centuries |

## 3. THE PEOPLE

- **The judges** — who had a gallery built so they could watch.
- **Adrianus Blom** — see §7.

---

## 4. THE ACTION — five different kinds of confinement in one building

The sequence 1556 → 1598 → 1655 → 1672 is the story, because each addition is a different
**theory of punishment**, built in stone, one after another:

1. **1556 — the cell block.** Hold people.
2. **1598 — the tuchthuis.** Reform people through forced labour.
3. **1655 — the spin- en rasphuis.** Extract labour: spinning, and rasping hardwood for dye.
4. **1672 — the vierschaar.** Judge people, formally, on site.
5. **The square outside.** Kill people, publicly.

One building contains the entire apparatus. A visitor can stand in front of it and be shown
four centuries of changing ideas about what to do with people who break the law — and the 1556
cells are **still there**.

## 7. ⚠️ EDITORIAL DECISION REQUIRED — the 1856 case

The last execution in Leiden is fully documented, and the detail is harrowing.

**Adrianus Blom** (b. 7 April 1824, Rietveld; d. 9 July 1856, Leiden), a farm labourer, was
sentenced to death in 1856 and hanged on the **Pieterskerkhof**. He was among the last people
executed in the Netherlands in peacetime. On **3 February 1856** he broke into the house of his
former employer **Cornelis van Velsen** and was discovered by the daughter, **Maria van Velsen**,
aged eighteen. The sources record that she offered to keep the break-in secret if he left, that
he said he could not leave a witness alive, and that after a struggle he killed her.

**This is a real murdered teenager, named, with the manner of her death on record.**

The question is not whether it is true — it is verified — but **how much of it belongs in a
tourist app**, and in whose interest it is told.

**Claude's recommendation, for the user to accept or overrule:**
- **Name both people.** Maria van Velsen should not be an anonymous victim in the story of
  Leiden's last execution; naming her is the more respectful choice, not the less.
- **State plainly** that Blom killed her during a burglary and was hanged for it in 1856.
- **Do not describe the killing.** The struggle, the blows and the drowning are documented and
  do not need to be narrated to a visitor on a phone.
- **No AI reconstruction of the crime or the execution** — extend DEC-011's logic here.
- Lead with what the location is about — the machinery of punishment — not with the murder.

**This is flagged, not decided.** It belongs in `R02`'s output as an explicit editorial ruling.

## 9. R05 — UNCERTAINTY REGISTER

- Built early 13th c.; comital court with residence, chapel (later the Pieterskerk) and orchard.
- A 13th-century tower served as the comital prison.
- **Donated to the city in the 15th century.**
